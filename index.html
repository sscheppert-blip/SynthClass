How do I use a synthesizer?

Turn the knobs and hear what changes. Step by step, discover how waveforms, pulse width, filters and envelopes shape your sound. Explore everything using a mouse, touch or your computer keyboard.

Before you start
SOUND ONClick “SOUND ON” on the synth and play a key. Your browser only enables audio after a click or key press.
PLAYClick the piano keys or use your computer keyboard. Each piano key shows its matching letter. X and C change the octave.
EXAMPLESSound examples stop automatically. To explore for longer, choose a sound in the synth and click “TEST (LOOP)”. Click “STOP” to end playback.
KNOBSDrag a knob up or down to change its value. Double-click to reset it. You can also focus a knob and use the arrow keys.

Chapter 2 · OSC section

The oscillator makes the sound

Picture someone humming “mmmmm” without ever stopping. That is the oscillator. TUNE makes it hum higher or lower. The selector decides how it hums: the waveform. Each shape sounds different because it brings different overtones. Those are quiet extra tones above the fundamental that make a sound bright or soft.

Watch both screens while you try them: on the left the wave itself, on the right its overtones as a grey mountain range.

Sine

SIN

Smooth and round, like whistling. Just the fundamental, no overtones.

Good for: sub bass, 808, soft flutes.

Triangle

TRI

Like a sine with a few corners. Soft, slightly hollow.

Good for: flutes, gentle leads, round basses.

Sawtooth

SAW

Bright, buzzy, full. Contains every overtone, which makes it the best food for the filter.

Good for: leads, supersaws, strings, acid.

Square

SQR

Hollow and nasal like a clarinet. Only every other overtone is present.

Good for: basses, chiptune, organ leads.

Noise

NOI

No pitch, just hiss. Like a radio between stations.

Good for: hi-hats, snares, wind, risers.

Pulse width · PW

A square wave spends half of each period high and half low: 50 % pulse width. The PW knob changes that ratio. At 10 %, the pulse is short and the gap is long. This changes the overtones and tone colour while the fundamental pitch stays the same.

PW only affects the square/pulse waveform (SQR) here. Select SQR and turn PW by hand: narrow pulses sound thinner and more nasal, while 50 % sounds hollow. PW has no effect on sine, triangle, sawtooth or noise.

Chapter 4 · FILTER section

The filter takes something away

The oscillator usually delivers too much: too bright, too buzzy. The filter is like a hand in front of your mouth or a blanket over the speaker. It only lets part of the sound through.

CUTOFF says where the hand is. RES (resonance) makes the filter whistle right at that spot; turned all the way up it squeals. The right screen shows the white filter curve and how it cuts into the grey overtone mountains.

With “Low-pass, almost closed”: slowly open CUTOFF all the way. That “wooaaah” is a filter sweep, half of every trance build-up.

Low-pass

LP

Lows pass, highs get dampened. Sounds muffled, like music from the flat next door.

Good for: basses, pads, acid, sweeps.

High-pass

HP

The other way round: highs pass, the bass gets thrown out. Sounds thin, like a tiny radio.

Good for: cleaning bass out of pads, build-ups.

Band-pass

BP

Only a strip in the middle remains. Sounds like a telephone or a megaphone.

Good for: nasal leads, wah, percussion.

12 or 24 dB says how quickly the hand closes. 12 dB per octave is gentle, a lot still gets through. 24 dB is steep and fat; the famous Moog filter is a 24 dB low-pass.

Chapter 5 · AMP ENV section

Amp and envelope

Amp means amplifier, in a synth also called VCA. It is the door between oscillator and speaker. The oscillator is always humming, remember. Without the amp you would hear it all the time, even with no key pressed.

An envelope is a hand that makes exactly the same movement on every key press. At the amp it opens and closes the door. You set the movement with four knobs. The little screen in the section shows the shape; the bar on the right shows where the hand is right now.

A · Attack

How fast does the door open when you press a key? Short is snappy, long swells in.

D · Decay

How fast does it then fall to the holding level?

S · Sustain

How loud does it stay while you hold the key? This is a level, not a time.

R · Release

How long does it ring on after you let go? Zero means gone instantly.

The sustain example plays for six seconds. Turn AMP ENV S, then hear the release set by R. For more time, choose a sound and press TEST; STOP ends the loop.

Chapter 6 · FILTER ENV section

Same hand, different knob

The filter envelope has the same four knobs but turns CUTOFF instead of the volume. How far it turns is set by ENV AMT in the filter section. At zero it does nothing at all. Every note becomes bright first and then dull: “wow”.

While the acid line is running: turn CUTOFF, RES and the D of the FILTER ENV. That is exactly how acid gets played live.
